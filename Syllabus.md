## Course Description

This intensive 10-day field course investigates the microbial drivers of the Adirondack ecosystem. Students design and conduct field experiments examining key microbial metabolisms—phototrophy, nitrogen fixation, sulfate reduction, methanogenesis, iron oxidation—along with cultivation-based studies of antimicrobial resistance, biofilm formation, and insect-associated microbiomes. Working in two teams of three, students develop hypotheses, collect and analyze data using base R, and communicate findings through oral presentations and a scientific report. The course bridges Winogradsky's tradition of environmental observation with Koch's tradition of pure culture.

---

## Learning Outcomes

### Skill Objectives

- Field sampling methods for water, soil, sediment, and plant-associated microbes
- Aseptic technique and parallel cultivation on selective and differential media
- Compound light microscopy including wet mounts and Gram staining to characterize microbial morphology
- Hypothesis development and experimental design with appropriate controls
- Data analysis in base R
- Scientific writing in IMRaD format
- Oral communication of experimental results

### Knowledge Objectives

- Connect microbial metabolisms to the chemical and physical gradients that select for them
- Understand the strengths and limitations of cultivation-based vs. direct observation approaches to microbial ecology
- Recognize how microbes alter their environment and structure adjacent communities
- Identify some ecologically and clinically important microbial groups by morphology and cultivation behavior
- Apply biogeochemical principles to predict where particular metabolisms will be found

### Lab Notebook

Your notebook is the main evidence that you are (1) performing experiments correctly, (2) documenting observations accurately, and (3) interpreting results thoughtfully. Even if your experimental work is excellent, partial credit will be assigned if observations and interpretations are incomplete or disorganized. The notebook will be collected at the midpoint of the course (end of Week 1) and again at the end. Please ensure your name is on the notebook.

### Individual Report

Each student writes a 4–5 page (single-spaced) report on one exercise or system. Methods sections may be shared across team members (since the methods were genuinely shared), but each shared section must explicitly attribute author contributions following CRediT (Contributor Roles Taxonomy) conventions. Introduction, Results, Discussion, and Conclusions sections are strictly independent work.

### Team Presentation

Each team delivers a 30-minute presentation: a brief joint introduction framing the team's overall question; three individual segments (~7 minutes each) reporting on one exercise per team member; and a joint synthesis connecting the segments to broader themes. Presentations are on the morning of July 17.

## Working in Teams

Six students will be split into two teams of three. Each team works through the same exercises in parallel; teams compare results during synthesis discussions. Within a team, each student takes lead authorship on one exercise or system for the individual report. Lead authorship rotates so that every student is the principal author of exactly one piece of work and a contributing author on the others.

In-lab collaboration is encouraged and often necessary for time-sensitive procedures. However, interpretation and discussion in the individual report are strictly independent work. Identical answers will receive no credit.

Students may use Claude or other AI tools to support data analysis, learn R syntax, troubleshoot statistical approaches, and discuss conceptual questions. *AI tools may not be used to write the individual report or the body of any submitted text.* Hypothesis statements, results interpretation, and discussion must be your own work. When AI tools are used to generate or check code, document this in your notebook and report following CRediT conventions, specifying the tool used, the prompt, and how the output was modified.
